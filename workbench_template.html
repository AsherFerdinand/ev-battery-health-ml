Questions this investigation answers

When can Hybrid B help or hurt?

In the independent v2.0 Thevenin cohort, original Hybrid B reduced mean SOC MAE to 0.663 pp from 0.708 pp for fixed EKF. On the diffusion cohort it increased MAE to 7.639 pp from 7.527 pp.

Can voltage fit improve while SOC worsens?

Yes. On the diffusion cohort Hybrid B voltage RMSE was 17.07 mV versus 17.72 mV for fixed EKF, while SOC MAE was higher. A voltage residual is not a direct measurement of SOC error.

What did the safeguard accept?

It rejected every adaptation on both independent final cohorts and exactly tied fixed EKF. It rejected beneficial as well as harmful updates, so it is not a validated selective reliability detector.

What remains unverified experimentally?

NASA discharge capacity supports capacity-derived SOH, but the records provide no independent SOC truth. Experimental SOC accuracy, calibrated voltage residuals and adaptive-parameter identifiability remain unverified.

The capacity study also found endpoint-phase differences can push predictions against clipping bounds. Read the exported diagnostics and linked v2.0 report for cohort-specific evidence.

    Measured inputs and held-out errors

    Use samples through 180 s, inclusive. Features summarize start voltage, voltage change and spread, current level and spread, integrated charge, mean temperature and temperature change. Available capacity is nominal capacity × predicted SOH.

    Reading the errors: MAE is the average absolute prediction error; RMSE penalises larger misses more strongly. Both here are SOH percentage points (pp). Lower values are generally better. Error standard deviation describes consistency, not accuracy; bias shows overprediction or underprediction.

    Technical details and limits

    NASA cells shown here are B0005, B0006, B0007 and B0018. The project defines its capacity-derived reference as measured discharge capacity divided by a 2 Ah nominal reference. That convention is not a claim about manufacturer-rated capacity. The ML view evaluates predicted SOH against this capacity-derived reference. NASA does not provide an independent SOC ground-truth trajectory in these records. This drawing is generic and does not assert the test cell chemistry, manufacturer or dimensions.

    The simulator supplies reference SOC and SOH. The three noisy sensor traces are the estimator inputs.

    The recorded channels are terminal voltage (V), current (A), cell temperature (°C) and elapsed time (s). Discharge capacity (Ah) is measured at test completion; this project derives SOH by dividing it by its stated 2 Ah reference.

    Voltage changes with SOC, load and internal dynamics; it may recover after current falls during rest, which is not evidence of charging. Temperature reflects the recorded cell sensor. NASA current polarity remains as recorded, so discharge is typically negative. Discharge, charge and rest labels are inferred from sampled current using −0.05 A and +0.05 A thresholds. These records do not provide independent SOC truth.

    Engineering consideration

    Charge and ageing example

    A separate educational illustration; it does not change the simulation above or predict ageing.

    SOH sets the usable-capacity ceiling.

    SOC is the fraction of that currently usable capacity which remains charged. Lower SOH does not make the case smaller.

    At 80% capacity SOH and 50% SOC, the simplified remaining charge is 0.8 × 0.5 = 40% of nominal capacity.

    Why batteries warm under load

    Current through internal resistance produces heat: Ploss = I²R. Heat transfer, thermal mass, cooling and operating conditions also affect temperature. The thermometer above is the existing lumped ECM thermal state, not a precise cell-temperature prediction.

    Capacity can fade as active material or cyclable lithium is lost. Resistance may also rise, but these changes depend on chemistry, use and ageing mechanism and need not follow one fixed ratio.
